## Problem and intended behavior

The promoted source still recorded zero full LT08 passes in its eight official lab games, with mean partial credit 0.92. Those are i34-9's measurements, not mine. In my new public baseline of that exact promoted source, the lab also failed: partial 0.8703. By day 38 it had 42 citizens, 23 placed beds and 12 free beds in stock. Repeated placement calls targeted the full 9x7 A8 floor and returned `no_space`. The final predicate failed both bed coverage and drink growth. Supplies remained positive and no citizen died.

The port repairs the mechanical chain before the hands-off window: reserve room floors for housing, manufacture enough beds early, place them within supported site bounds, and give the layer a pass every one or two managed days. Its first isolated version retained fa9c13cc's brew parameters. All three public cases then had enough built beds, no deaths and continuous food/drink, but all three still lost drink across the window. This separate result motivated the managed-window brew change; housing alone was insufficient.

On open runs, the port also repairs a concrete office geometry problem observed in a public full-year trace. The manager owned an empty office on one level while built chairs were two levels below. An isolated earlier repair successfully created an office over a known built chair and the actual API returned `chair: true` on assignment. The combined candidate uses that same factual test; it does not infer a usable chair from aggregate chair stock.

## Changes relative to the promoted source

1. **Housing room reservation on large managed tasks.** With positive `hands_off_in_days` and at least 25 citizens, after two farm plots or assigned farm rooms, unused room sites are reserved for the missing dormitory, dining and office roles. Room roles are still recorded only after a successful dig. The farm-floor candidates skip the levels already reserved for those housing roles. Existing farm tasks continue through the normal public tools; no room observation is fabricated or rewritten.

2. **Manufacturing within the actual order limit.** The normal bed order may batch up to 30 jobs on that same managed population/clock condition. Thirty is the server's real maximum for `add_order`; software checks use its actual converter, including larger hypothetical populations. While aggregate bed stock is below citizens, replacement chair and table batches wait if at least one of each already exists. A first chair/table remains eligible for the office. The promoted source's surge target of citizens plus 12 is retained. Its second-Carpenters step remains in its closer; the port does not add another copy of that step earlier in the pass.

3. **Bounded public-site placement and zoning.** The ported managed furnishing calls preserve the observed site ID/origin and cap optional width/height at 40; its office and furnishing-zone calls cap them at 20. Coverage is checked against the same bounded zone rectangle. Dimensions never exceed the observed site. The actual public `SiteState.resolve` preserves these optional bounds, and real native API calls exercised the bounded office and furniture paths. No arbitrary coordinate or invented site fallback is introduced. The public observation remains unchanged.

4. **A working manager office.** Managed offices use a known built chair inside a valid bounded site, or place an available chair within the exact new office footprint before assigning it. Tiny stair areas are skipped. Failed zone/chair calls do not assign a new empty office. A previously observed chaired office can be handed to a replacement manager on the existing large-managed trigger. On an open run the new repair branch applies only when a built chair is visibly inside a usable bounded public site; otherwise the promoted source's ordinary office path remains. A ready office is retained, and an already owned office covering a real chair prevents repeated re-zoning even if the administrators summary reports an older empty office first.

5. **Managed advance cadence.** A model-requested advance while the hands-off countdown is positive is capped at two days, or one day while digging is pending or at most four managed days remain. This gives the daily controller manufacturing/placement opportunities before the window. The inherited layer-only fallback already uses one/two-day quiet advances. Open-run model advances keep the inherited 14-day maximum.

6. **Managed-window brewing parameters.** Inside the inherited one-time protected `o_brew_win` order, a managed fort with at least 25 citizens uses four daily brew jobs, two brewable plants as the floor, and drink cap `max(600, 32 * citizens)`. Smaller labs retain the promoted source's five-job order, half-a-plant-per-citizen reserve and 2000 cap. The inherited extra barrel order and ordinary butchering remain. No duplicate new late-window order is added: acceptance still marks the existing closer key done and records the order as layer owned.

The STATUS digest, PLAYBOOK, action/failure memory, owned-order and civilian-alert guards, re-zoning, endgame supply closer, wood-crafts progression, credit fallback, fixed-model chat settings, Docker base digest and nonroot entrypoint are inherited from fa9c13cc. Four idle turns still cause a one-day advance. At five failed model turns or 92% credits, the inherited program continues with its layer instead of abandoning the fort. This candidate does not add the separate visible-unit context experiment, the slope-triggered animal reserve experiment, an automatic labor allocator, or a new wealth-order cancellation policy.

## Limits and interpretation

The lower plant threshold can compete with edible plant supply; the native continuous-food clause is checked explicitly. Manufacturing orders, placed jobs and completed built furniture are distinct, so only the actual scorer's built-bed predicate establishes a pass. Site bounds are rectangles, not an assertion that every tile is usable or safe. Failed placement is still an ordinary API refusal; this candidate adds no alternate-area placement after `no_space`.

The inherited crafts order remains a standing order while logs satisfy its reserve: the target stops creating a new order, not an already accepted repeat. That inherited behavior is retained. The model and controller can still disagree, and any official gain must clear the benchmark's actual score and paired-promotion rules. Only an official promoted result establishes success. Further work continues after this attempt regardless of its result.
